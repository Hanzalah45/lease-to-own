What shipped

Serial number in, ownership out

Milestone 4 is Phase 1 of equipment tracking: the simple tracking fields, the serial-number workflow, and a data model laid out so a GPS provider can be added later without rebuilding anything. Live GPS is Phase 2 and is deliberately not built — the client supplies the provider when that phase starts.

Backend — Laravel API

  • Serial-number inventory, fully managed Add, edit, view and remove units. Search matches serial number, model or VIN; the list filters by status and reports live counts across the fleet.
  • Assign and release — the link to a customer Assigning is the single point where a serial number becomes tied to a lease. It stamps the lease, marks the unit leased, and works out the expected ownership date from the lease term automatically.
  • Four-stage lifecycle In stock, leased, returned, or owned by the customer once the lease is paid off. A paid-off unit keeps its lease link so the history stays attached; a returned unit is detached and freed for the next customer.
  • Service history per unit Date, work performed, and the staff member who logged it — taken from the signed-in account rather than the form, so entries cannot be attributed to someone else.
  • Customer-facing view, read-only Scoped through the customer's own leases, so nobody can see another customer's serial numbers. Internal condition notes and GPS fields never leave the server.
  • Guard rails against bad data Duplicate serials refused; a unit already on a lease cannot be double-assigned or quietly switched; a unit with lease history cannot be deleted.

Frontend — Next.js

  • Equipment section in the admin dashboard Fleet counters at the top, search by serial number, and status filters — leased, in stock, returned, owned.
  • Unit detail page Tracking fields, who currently holds the unit and on which lease, and the full service log with inline add.
  • Assign and release dialogs Assign offers only leases that have no unit on them; release asks what actually happened to the machine — returned, back in stock, or paid off.
  • My Equipment in the customer portal The customer sees their own machine: model, serial number, delivery date, expected ownership date, and when it was last serviced.
  • Dashboard panel now shows real data The equipment panel on the admin home screen held placeholder content; it now reads the live fleet.
  • Validation on every field Serial numbers, dates and notes are checked as you type and the save button stays disabled until the form is valid — the same behaviour as the customer forms, now applied across the dashboard.

Verified, not just built

  1. A unit is added to inventory, assigned to a live lease, and the ownership date comes out correct from the lease term — a 24-month lease delivered 2 September 2026 returned 2 September 2028.
  2. Search finds a machine by serial number alone; status filters and fleet counts match the database.
  3. Every guard was tested and holds: duplicate serial refused, a leased unit cannot be reassigned or have its status edited behind the workflow, and a unit with lease history cannot be deleted.
  4. An admin limited to equipment access can do the whole job; an admin without it is refused by the API itself, not merely hidden in the interface.
  5. A customer is refused on every admin route, and sees only the machine on their own lease.
  6. Service records are logged against the correct unit and attributed to the signed-in staff member.

Built for later

Phase 2 GPS — the groundwork, not the feature

The brief asked for the data model to be structured for a Phase 2 GPS provider. That structure is in place and costs nothing today.

A device field on every unit. Each machine can hold a GPS device ID now, so units can be tagged as hardware is fitted — nothing reads it yet.
A location history table, empty by design. Ready to receive location pings, geofence status and tamper alerts. Nothing writes to it in Phase 1, so it adds no weight to the system.
No provider assumed. Whichever provider is chosen, it connects to the model already built rather than forcing a rebuild of equipment tracking.

Worth a decision

One thing to settle before Milestone 6

Found while testing this milestone end to end. It is not a fault in the work delivered — it is a question about how two earlier pieces should meet, and it needs your call rather than a guess.

The application form creates its own machine. When an application is submitted, whatever make, model and serial were typed into the form become a brand-new equipment record, instead of picking a machine out of the inventory built in this milestone. Two consequences: the fleet gradually fills with placeholder entries when the serial field is left blank, and the assign step is bypassed because the lease already has a unit attached.
Option A — pick from inventory. The application form offers the machines already in stock instead of typing a serial. Cleanest result: one record per real machine, and inventory always reflects reality. A little more work, as the form and the service behind it both change.
Option B — a swap action. The form stays as it is, and the equipment screen gains a swap action that releases the placeholder and assigns the real machine. Faster to deliver, but it leaves a manual step for staff to remember.
Recommendation. Option A, folded into Milestone 6 when the admin screens are being reworked anyway — no throwaway work, and staff never have to remember a cleanup step.
